## Review

Much shorter and much colder than [[Dune]]. Twelve years after the first book, Paul is emperor and
hates what his name has become. The ideas are great (prescience as a trap you can see but not leave),
but most of the book is people in rooms, plotting in riddles. I respected it more than I enjoyed it.

## Quotes

> There exists no separation between gods and men; one blends softly casual into the other.

## Notes

- Read it as the second half of the first book; Herbert apparently meant it as a corrective.
- The ending landed. The middle dragged.
- Moving straight on to [[Children of Dune]] while the setting is fresh.
